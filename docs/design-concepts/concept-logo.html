Flix marks

Three directions, each drawn on the Projection Room palette and shown where it actually has to survive: 16px in a browser tab, in the navbar lockup over both solid ground and a hero scrim, and as an app tile against backgrounds we do not control. Pick one direction — the variants inside it are mine to settle.

Direction A

Projection beam

The literal north star: a lens throwing light rightward into the dark. The wedge is the only amber shape, so the mark needs no plate and no second colour — it can sit directly on the ground the app already paints.

Two cuts

Solid wedge (left) holds at any size. Split rays (right) read as film light above ~32px and turn to mush below it — usable for the marketing header, not the favicon.

For

  • Only direction that carries the "Projection Room" idea, not generic cinema.
  • No plate — works on ground, on a hero scrim, and inside the wordmark line.
  • Asymmetric silhouette; distinguishable from every square app icon in a dock.

Against

  • The gradient falloff flattens at 16px; the tab icon is effectively a solid wedge.
  • Amber-only means it leans on the accent for identity — brushes the three-job rule.
  • Lives one wrong curve away from a megaphone — the outward-arced far edge is what keeps it a beam, and it has to stay.

Direction B

The amber period

Promotes the punctuation the wordmark already ships. The dot is the projected light; the hairline bar left of it is the wordmark cropped to its last stroke. Mark and wordmark stop being two things.

Two cuts

With the baseline bar (left) the mark says "…x." — quiet, typographic. Without it (right) it is a lamp on a plate: cleaner at 16px, but it stops referencing the wordmark and becomes a generic dot.

Direction C

Film gate

The frame the light passes through: punched sprockets on both edges, amber aperture at the centre. Cinema-literal and instantly legible — the conventional answer, drawn on our palette and our two radii.

Two cuts

Square aperture (left) echoes the 0.75rem plate radius everywhere else in the app. Round aperture (right) reads as a lens and separates better from the sprockets at small sizes.

Wordmark treatment

My call

A, solid wedge — with the hairline wordmark

B and C both hide behind a plate, and a plate is what an app reaches for when its mark cannot hold the ground on its own. A holds it. It is also the only one of the three that could not belong to a different app: B is a dot, C is a film gate, A is this app's stated idea drawn once. Its real cost is the amber dependency — the mark becomes a fourth job for the accent, which DESIGN.md forbids without removing one. That is a one-line amendment to the Three-Job Amber Rule ("…and the mark"), and it should be made deliberately rather than by drift.
